# Data Shape Notes

## Karmin 2015
- Main empirical asymmetry paper.
- Y bottleneck occurs in a bounded late-Holocene / post-Neolithic interval.
- mtDNA does not show comparable collapse.
- Karmin reports the Y plot suggests a reduction around 8–4 kya.
- Female effective size is reported as up to 17-fold higher than male in that interval.
- Figure 2 is the key asymmetry figure:
  - cumulative Bayesian skyline plots of Y chromosome and mtDNA diversity by world regions
  - strongest empirical anchor for bounded male-line compression
- Figure 1 is useful topologically:
  - Y phylogeny with collapsed clades and geographic composition
  - useful for asking whether the tree itself feels sparse / pruned in the bottleneck interval

## Poznik 2016
- Main survivor-burst paper.
- Abstract explicitly reports "bursts of extreme expansions in male numbers."
- The important shape here is not direct observation of pruning, but concentrated expansion among surviving male lines.
- Figure 2 is the key survivor-burst figure:
  - Y-chromosome phylogeny and haplogroup distribution
  - dotted triangles represent ages and sample sizes of expanding lineages
- Useful comparison target:
  - model survivor-expansion / post-pruning topology
- Also important:
  - such extreme expansions are much less evident in mtDNA

## Yan/Wang 2013
- Concrete regional case of concentrated survivor expansion.
- Three strong star-like Neolithic expansions at ~6 kya.
- Roughly 40% of modern Chinese patrilines descend from these three lines.
- Figure 1 is the key figure:
  - phylogenetic tree emphasizing the three star-like expansions (Oα, Oβ, Oγ)
- Useful as a regional exemplar of post-pruning survivor expansion.
- Not direct observation of pruning, but strong evidence of concentrated expansion by a few surviving lines.

## Guyon 2024
- Important comparison point for non-war explanation.
- Peaceful patrilineal segmentary system proposed as sufficient to generate substantial male effective-population reduction.
- Important because it explicitly addresses the Karmin asymmetry.
- Useful figure:
  - skyline-style comparison of male and female effective population sizes and female-to-male ratio
- Important textual point:
  - violence is argued to be neither sufficient nor necessary to explain the bottleneck
- Useful as literature support that non-violent structural mechanisms are now respectable, not eccentric.

## Working empirical claim
- The reported data do not directly observe "punctuated pruning" as a historical chronicle.
- What they do show is a reconstructed pattern consistent with it:
  - bounded interval of male-line compression
  - no comparable mtDNA collapse
  - burst-like / star-like expansion of a few surviving patrilines
- "Punctuated pruning" is my descriptive term for that reconstructed pattern, not a standard term from the literature.

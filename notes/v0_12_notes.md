# v0.12 Daisyworld Notes

## Purpose

v0.12 is the first architectural move beyond the single-basin mean-field world.

The goal is not “more realism” in the vulgar sense. The goal is to ask whether some of the observed global bounded-compression shape arises more naturally once lineages inhabit several partially closed reproductive fields rather than one universal bowl.

## Architecture

Three basins.
Weak symmetric coupling.
Staggered local tightening windows.
Shared internal machinery.

These basins are not necessarily geographic. They are partially bounded social reproductive fields.

## What improved

Compared with the single-basin family, v0.12 improves the global story:

- aggregate bounded compression looks less forced
- pre-window global loss is lower
- window-dominated global loss is clearer
- aggregate peak pruning generation moves materially rightward
- survivor monopoly weakens
- diversity is preserved more naturally

This is already enough to show that the single-basin world was topologically too polite.

## What did not improve enough

The local basin story is not yet right.

Current issue:
- basin-specific peak pruning still occurs too early relative to each basin’s own local window

So the aggregate improvement seems to arise more from superposition of several similar local declines than from cleanly staggered local transitions.

That is an important distinction.

## Current interpretation

v0.12 supports the claim that some of what looked like scalar “damage” in the single-basin model was really missing topology.

But topology alone does not yet solve:
- local timing
- durable post-window scar
- or rebound

So Daisyworld is currently:
- an architectural success
- a local calibration problem
- and a warning against overloading scalar hysteresis terms with work that partially belongs to structure

## Immediate lesson

The machine is still too much of a globe and not enough of an archipelago.

v0.12 is the first honest correction of that, but not the last.
